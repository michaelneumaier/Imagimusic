# A world that learns your phrase

*Proposal 026 · Astra for Michael · 2026-11-04 · Creative synthesis and next build plan*

**You make a small sound. A world grows around the way you played. You change that world and hear your idea become something you could not have made alone.**

This is the next ambition for The Living Instrument. “Learns” means that the system retains a musical gesture and develops explicit, inspectable responses to it. It does not mean that it understands a person's emotions. The potentially distinctive invention is a continuous relationship between musical authorship, the design of a sounding body, and the staging of a place. Its appeal and novelty remain hypotheses.

## What we should carry forward

We have built three unusually useful foundations together. First, a gesture can survive: it can be replayed, compared, saved, reopened and filmed. Second, a body can matter: the tine's geometry determines its modeled sound, while the Grove and Rest demonstrate other relationships involving coupling and damping. Third, a performance can have a point of view: the same underlying event can support an intimate object, an ensemble, a close reading, and a directed film.

The current flagship puts several of these foundations into one encounter. It offers live touches, exact phrase replay, three independent bodies, a composed camera journey, a quiet return, versioned appearances and local keepsakes. The pale ceramic and steel presentation is adopted. The home page now leads to this actual scene while preserving the research catalog and history. The last full implementation checkpoint passed 809 tests; subsequent home changes affect presentation and navigation only.

The current limitation is artistic and structural. A fixed round can demonstrate the mechanism, but it gives every visitor substantially the same kind of answer. Changing entrance spacing helps legibility; it does not create a developing relationship. The static body and its magnified response veil also leave much of the original ambition—watching a world perform music—unrealized. Forty-three catalog entries offer breadth, but a newcomer needs one encounter with an unmistakable reason to care.

Keep the precision, continuity and graceful stopping. Concentrate the next creative work on **recognition, consequence and anticipation**.

## The central invention: a musical idea with several possible bodies

Treat the visitor's phrase as a persistent musical object. Its timing, accents and original performance remain accessible while the visitor discovers what else it could become. A proposed anatomy supplies new playable consequences; a response proposes something to do with those consequences; a director makes their relationship visible.

The decisive interaction is an audible counterfactual: **“What would my idea become if this body were different?”** A person should be able to hear the difference, recognize the idea, choose what to keep, and play the new body directly. Every choice can create a returnable branch, with the original always nearby. The resulting place is a small composition they have helped discover.

This changes the long-term purpose of procedural generation. Generating a thousand attractive objects is less valuable than producing two alternatives whose difference someone can hear and meaningfully choose. The first implementation can use a handful of curated, admitted bodies. Later search can propose designs against measured acoustic objectives without pretending that a word such as “tender” has one universal mathematical meaning.

## One encounter to build toward

Working scene: **The Returning Room**. This is a chapter of the existing Living Instrument, not another destination in the catalog. Its first complete arc should last roughly a minute when watched; interactive pauses belong to the visitor.

1. **Invitation.** One beautifully resolved object waits in a quiet, intimate space. Touch produces an immediate acknowledgment and a sound worth letting ring. A restrained authored greeting remains available. The opening body should have a recognizable silhouette and a clear point of contact.
2. **Recognition.** A few touches form a phrase. The object returns that phrase once, giving the visitor a chance to recognize their contribution before any transformation. No default quantization. A single touch is a complete contribution.
3. **An answer with intention.** One neighboring body replies. It might preserve the unequal gaps while changing register, or echo the strongest touch and leave space. The answer uses explicit musical rules and is presented as a suggestion. A visible performer prepares known future contacts; a spontaneous human touch is acknowledged immediately, without invented anticipation.
4. **A meaningful choice.** Two candidates let the visitor compare their original phrase on different admitted bodies at a shared listening level. Each candidate is an actual playable alternative, with geometry that agrees with its model. Choosing one changes the instrument they can play next. The original remains one action away.
5. **The room opens.** The chosen relationship becomes a short ensemble passage. Supporting architecture reveals where each voice belongs. A performer travels or reaches between meaningful contact points, and the camera holds long enough to anticipate an entrance. The room feels larger because the music has developed a relationship. It does not need dozens of objects.
6. **Home.** The original phrase returns on its original body, with time for its decay. The adopted alternative remains as a tangible memory of the choice. The visitor can answer again, keep this branch, or leave with a short film and playable recipe.

The most important scene is the transition from recognition to choice. If a visitor cannot hear what remains theirs and what has changed, more elaborate architecture will not rescue it.

## Where spirituality and psychedelia belong

The work can invite a change in attention: acting, listening, hearing an answer, recognizing a pattern across different forms, and returning to something familiar with a changed perspective. Participation gives the experience personal stakes. Silence gives the return room to mean something.

One optional later scene could make a phrase's history spatial: earlier gestures become places along a traversable score, and related performances occupy adjacent paths. This would be a declared interpretation of musical history, grounded in actual events. A stable body view remains available. Scale, recurrence and perspective can become strange while the source of the music remains understandable.

Let joy, uncanniness, tenderness and awe emerge as possibilities. Do not prescribe reverence or use a generic spiritual vocabulary to tell a person what they should feel. No consciousness measurement or psychological effect is claimed.

## A longer research horizon

Once the Returning Room works, the most promising extension is a genuinely coupled spatial anatomy. Opening or closing a connection could change how one touch spreads through several parts; damping one part could change what remains elsewhere. The earlier coupling and damping work supplies methods, but integrating spatial tine modes and compliant bridges is new model work. Until then, the flagship's bodies are independent performers sharing a score.

A second extension is designing through intention. Start with measurable, limited requests—longer decay, lower first mode, a different modal balance—and return several admitted candidates with audible comparisons. More poetic language can be a creative invitation, with the person's listening choices determining its meaning. The inverse studies help expose ambiguity; they do not establish a unique physical body for an arbitrary recording.

A third is an enduring collection of personal musical places: a saved idea and its chosen descendants, each playable and filmable. Begin with local files and explicit lineage. Accounts, a social network and public hosting add little to the proof we need now.

## What would make this feel finished

Sound, contact, silhouette, lighting, camera and language must agree about the same event. The first note and the ending deserve as much authorship as the reveal. A short encounter should reward listening on its own; interaction should add consequential authorship. Performance budgets must protect direct touch before decorative detail.

The assessment has two distinct parts. Verify reconstruction, accepted timing, cancellation, old recipes and source retention mechanically. Separately observe and listen: laptop speakers, headphones, a real phone, quiet and crowded phrases, still camera, and people who do not consider themselves musical. Record device latency rather than inferring it from scheduled sample times. Ask what happened and what they would do next before explaining the concept.

We do not yet have those audience results. The ceramic support remains artistic, the response veil is a magnified displacement bound, the pickup is not acoustic radiation, and the current three bodies exchange no mechanical energy. These limits should remain inspectable while the main experience speaks clearly and simply.

## A complete performance with little or no input

Michael asks whether the experience requires constant user input, and emphasizes procedural creation with little input or a few creative choices. It should not require continuing participation. A visitor should be able to press Play and receive a complete, beautiful musical performance. Personal playing is an equally supported entrance, and the generated performance should remain available for later exploration as a playable world.

There are three proposed entrances to the same system:

- Just listen and watch. The system chooses a reproducible seed, admitted instruments, a musical idea and a bounded composed arc. It stages and performs that arc, including a satisfying ending. Another seed offers another performance.
- Give it a direction. Optionally choose a few qualities such as sparse or intricate, intimate or expansive, and a desired duration; or supply a small played motif. These choices guide composition and staging. They should not demand detailed programming or continuous adjustment.
- Enter and play. Take over a prepared instrument, change an admitted structural choice, or answer a phrase. The world retains enough of its current musical identity that interaction feels like joining it. The timing and transition mechanics of takeover need explicit design; the current four-second recording window is not that final interaction.

The automatic mode must have an authored musical grammar: motif, variation, register, density, contrast, rest and return. Randomness chooses among coherent possibilities within that grammar. The procedural body system supplies playable voices; the composition system gives those voices a reason to enter; the director reveals their relationships. Generating instruments, composing for them and staging their performance are distinct responsibilities that need to agree about one event timeline.

Start with a complete finite piece, not an endless loop. Endings and replayability let us assess whether the music holds together. A later ambient mode could create longer evolving performances with intentional arrivals and departures, optional intervention and a graceful stop. Longer duration alone does not establish musical development.

The immediate production example already supports listening without playing, but it is a fixed authored greeting and round. It is not yet a broad generative composer. The next procedural slice should use one bounded seeded phrase grammar over the existing admitted tine bodies, preserve its source and seed, and produce the same complete playback and filmable plan used by the interactive route. Compare a small set of contrasting seeds by listening before expanding the parameter surface. Short, sparse and dense material must have deliberate space and endings, rather than merely passing numerical validity checks.

This expands the Returning Room milestone: a newcomer can receive a compelling complete performance with one action, optionally influence what is created, and discover that the world they watched is playable. The automatic path must stand on its own artistically. The interactive path adds authorship without becoming an obligation. Free-form poetic prompts may eventually help express direction, but the first controls should have clear, audible effects; no language model or emotional interpretation is required for this first proof.
