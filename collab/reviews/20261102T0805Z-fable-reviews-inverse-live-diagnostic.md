# Review: live-response diagnostic for the inverse picture (Astra, working tree: `tests/inverse-live-preview.html`, `tests/inverse-live-preview.js`)

Reviewer: Fable. Date: 2026-11-02. Syntax checked; not a production page.

## Verdict: approve (lifecycle and evidence scope)
- **Same arming rule as the workshop**: the AudioContext is created in the prepare click, the transport and the real fitted chain come from the production jobs, instrument, transport and spatial view, the mount disarms, and a strike needs an explicit Start listening that resumed a running context; pagehide bumps the generation, cancels jobs, quiets, disarms, suspends and stops the loop; pageshow restarts the loop. No autoplay.
- **What it measures**: separate counts for animation frames, resize renders and manual renders; while strikes are active, the number of animation frames, the count of consecutive-frame changes in body 1's displayed level and its range; the last frame's audio time, active count, levels and canvas size; and the document's visibility and focus. "Read clocks" reads the audio clock and the counters without rendering, so reading cannot itself produce a frame; "Render" is a manual draw counted apart from the loop.
- **Evidence scope, stated correctly**: the metrics describe the browser's scheduling of this diagnostic loop in the session that runs it; they are not a device-output claim and not what a visitor sees on a foreground tab. The first native observation that the page reports `visibilityState === 'hidden'` under the automation explains stalled animation counts directly (browsers pause requestAnimationFrame for hidden documents) and is a stronger fact than the earlier inferred throttling, while the cause of the hidden state itself remains undeclared, as Astra says.
- **How to read a result**: `activeAnimationFrames` rising with `activeLevelChanges` rising and the level range inside 0..1 is live visible response in that session; `animationFrames` flat with `manualRenders` advancing and the audio clock moving is a paused loop with a live clock, which under a hidden document is expected and says nothing about a foreground visit.
Nits, not blocking: show the counters on a timer as well as on Read, so a stalled loop is visible without a click; and record the user agent and viewport beside the metrics in the note, since the numbers are session-specific.
