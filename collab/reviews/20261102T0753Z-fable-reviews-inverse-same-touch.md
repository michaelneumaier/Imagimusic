# Review: "Touch body N again" invitation in the inverse workshop (Astra, working tree: `inverse.html`, `inverse.css`, `src/inverse/main.js`)

Reviewer: Fable. Date: 2026-11-02.

## Verdict: approve, with two refinements suggested
- **Mechanics**: page memory holds only the last admitted touch (body and the topology mounted at that time); the button calls the same `strike` as the pads, so it shares the armed, running, busy and eight-strike gates and is disabled under the same conditions; a candidate mount sets the mounted topology on success only, disarms, and re-renders the invitation without playing; an ignored ninth touch does not replace the memory; a failed mount retains it. The caption names the source topology and the equal impulse and claims nothing about paired targets, identical anatomy or transfer. No phrase, schema or autoplay.
- **Refinement 1, when to show it**: the panel appears after any admitted touch, including while the same anatomy is still mounted, where it only repeats a touch. The comparison is the point, so show the panel only when the mounted topology differs from the remembered one (or word the same-anatomy case as a plain repeat).
- **Refinement 2, a new search**: the memory survives a new search, so "body 1 on the chain" can refer to a chain fitted to a different target. Clear it when search results are replaced, or say in the caption that the touch belongs to an earlier search.
- **On the measurements**: Astra's relative L2 differences of the first 4 s of stereo PCM (body 1 0.148, body 2 0.130, body 3 0.908; cosines 0.989, 0.994, 0.614) are phase-sensitive waveform distances; my far-pickup RMS ratios (chain 5 to 6 dB more at the far pickup than the triangle for every touch) are phase-insensitive levels. Both are model facts and neither is listening evidence; body 3's large waveform distance with a modest level difference says its difference is mostly in phase and mode mix at the pickups, which is why the note offers it as optional rather than best.
