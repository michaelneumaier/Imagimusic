# Review: Clip structure and timing parser; comparison render harness, finalizer and artifact tests (Astra, working tree)

Reviewer: Fable. Date: 2026-11-02. Clip tests 7/7, artifact tests 2/2, full suite 579/579 here (run into a private file; the earlier "failing" result came from sharing `/tmp/suite.txt` with Astra's concurrent run, not from any test).

## Clip parser (`src/midi2/clip.js`, `tests/midi2-clip.test.js`): approve
Checked against M2-116-U §5 to §7 as read today: the SMF2CLIP header; the initial zero DCS and a nonzero DCTPQ as the first two packets (profile preambles refused as unsupported, named as a first-slice limit rather than a claim about the specification); DCTPQ once; the configuration tempo at most once, immediately after the DCTPQ at tick 0, and the time signature at most once after it; Start of Clip once with its own preceding DCS, End of Clip last with its own DCS and nothing after; consecutive clockstamps without an event refused; cumulative ticks and seconds kept for every packet with configuration time retained and the origin identified separately; musical seconds accumulated on their own so a large setup time cannot swallow small musical deltas; tempo records with their Group; the single-clock rule named as a receiver policy; same-tick cross-Group different values refused within a section; the two refinements accepted: the sequence's first tempo may replace the header's at the same absolute tick because they are different sections, and same-Group simultaneous tempos keep presentation order with the last applying forward; the 120 BPM default only reported when absent or when it governed elapsed time; unsupported packets listed with section, offset, message and raw words; limitations stated; no receiver state, admission, rendering or conformance claim.

## Render harness and finalizer (`tests/expressive-compare-render.html/js`, `tools/finalize-expressive-compare.mjs`, `tests/expressive-compare-artifacts.test.js`): approve
The harness refuses a comparison without `patchSnapshot`, verifies `patchSha256` as the hash of plain `JSON.stringify(patchSnapshot)`, builds each performance from the snapshot and not from the live default, renders each score twice and requires Float32-identical bytes, writes two WAV downloads and a report with source, patch, PCM and WAV hashes, frames, peak, RMS and the final 100 ms; nothing plays by itself. The finalizer independently re-derives the note-only score from the composed one, validates the saved patch, binds the browser report to the comparison and score bytes, checks WAV format fields, quantised peak and RMS and tail silence, and only then writes the shared player page; the artifact tests cover altered WAV bytes, a false repeat result, a changed note-only velocity hidden behind updated hashes and a wrong channel format. It states that it claims no independent Node render, which is right.

Producer side, now landed to match: `patch.json` is plain `JSON.stringify(validatePatch(DEFAULT_PATCH))` with nested fields intact and no newline, `comparison.json` carries `patchSnapshot`, `patchSha256` (hash of those exact bytes) and the file name; the top-level-key replacer that stripped nested fields is gone.
