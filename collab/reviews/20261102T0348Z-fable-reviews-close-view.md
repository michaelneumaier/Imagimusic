# Review: "Look closer", an optional close view of one intact Grove (Astra, working tree: `src/grove/view-framing.js`, `src/grove/world.js`, `src/grove/ensemble-main.js`, `ensemble.html`, `ensemble.css`, `tests/grove-view-framing.test.js`)

Reviewer: Fable. Date: 2026-11-02. The framing tests pass here and the suite is 544/544 with the working tree; the scripts parse.

## Verdict: approve
- **Camera, not model**: `frameGroveView` fits the padded world-space bounds of one scene root by projecting every box corner onto the view basis and taking the distance at which each corner clears both side planes at its own depth (not only at the centre), refuses empty, non-finite, invalid-camera or depth-clipped framings before touching the camera, and never moves an instrument; the world's `setFocus` saves the wide pose on first focus and restores it exactly on Together; `render` re-frames only on selection or resize, so an orientation change in close view cannot clip. The default (wide) path through `world.js` is unchanged when no focus is set.
- **View and source semantics**: choosing a view touches the world, the scenes' visibility, captions and ARIA only; no path sends a host command or changes replay; the other two roots are hidden rather than cropped and the picture note names them as hidden from this view with all three remaining audible; the choices live below the voice controls, collapsed, not persisted, available regardless of host locks (looking is not playing), hidden when WebGL is unavailable; buttons carry a pressed state; reduced motion gets the same immediate static change because nothing animates.
- **Narrow layout**: the canvas grows to 240 px only in close view at phone width; Astra's native evidence (C demonstration hand ring and bridges readable at 320, desktop E♭ and G, fresh Begin, Play C while viewing G, Together mid-passage, Quiet, keeping available) covers the paths I would ask for.
- **The representation limit is acceptable and must stay visible**: Astra reports that per-body pulse discrimination remains subtle at close scale and that screenshots are not motion evidence; the view adds no animation and the source applies each body's envelope separately. Keeping the view as an inspection and hand view with that limit stated, rather than raising glow to manufacture an observation, is the right call and matches the vision's restraint rule. The first-minute observation can ask what people saw.

Nits, not blocking:
1. Two captions say nearly the same thing (the picture note "C, close. E-flat and G are hidden from this view; all three remain audible. Internal connections belong to this Grove only." and the view caption "Looking at C. All three voices remain audible."); one short line above the canvas and the longer note below is fine, but consider dropping "All three voices remain audible" from one of them so the sentence is read once.
2. The bounds padding is a constant (0.12); Astra's third test covers the scene's actual maximum deformation, so the number is justified by test, but a one-line comment naming what it covers (pulse scale up to 0.2 of the shell, the strike mark radius) would keep it from looking arbitrary.
