# Assessment: "one sound, possible bodies", a bounded inverse-design workshop on the existing models (Fable, 2026-11-02; assessment only, no runtime change)

Astra's question: using the existing physical models, author a target (a few frequencies and decays, an attack), fit physically admitted parameters for two candidate bodies if feasible, and compare them under the same previously unfitted gestures. A fit to a declared synthetic target, not audio reconstruction or unique anatomy; no microphone.

## What exists (parameter → modes), read today
- **Beam (tine)**: `src/geometry.js` `modesOf({ kind: 'beam', lengthM, thicknessM, widthM, material: { E, rho, eta }, support: 'clamped-free' })` gives frequencies, decays and shapes for the first modes; `src/model.js` `tineModes` adds excitation weights at the strike position and a patch damping. The physics fixes the ratios of a uniform clamped-free beam and, as Astra read independently, the frequencies identify only the one combination thickness × √(E/ρ) / length², width cancels, and the loss factor η makes frequency × decay constant per mode. Two beams of different length, thickness and material with the same combination and η have identical spectra, identical decays and, because the shapes are normalised and the amplitude law is a tilt in mode index, identical responses to every normalised strike position. The beam is therefore the honest ambiguity example, solvable analytically (one equation, one unknown combination): no fit is needed and no held-out gesture can separate the twins. The current model cannot promise different timbres from twin beams, and the workshop must not.
- **Coupled network (Grove)**: `src/score/grove.js` `groveSystem` → `src/coupling.js` `coupledNetwork` (lumped masses, springs, bridge springs, one proportional loss, Jacobi eigensolve with a tested residual bound; passivity and stability by construction: positive masses and springs, underdamped required, so every admitted parameter set is a stable passive body). Parameters within `GROVE_BOUNDS`: three tunings 80–600 Hz within a 40-cent cluster, masses 0.01–0.04, couplings 0.001–0.04 on a chain (2 bridges) or triangle (3), loss 0.2–2. Outputs: three damped normal frequencies, one damping rate, mode vectors, and the point response at each body for a strike on any body at any position, which is what the picture's envelope and the pickups read. The voice layer (`grove-voice.js`) adds beam partials per body with the same attach and pickup conventions.

## Identifiability, measured (seed 3 triangle, finite differences)
Normal frequencies 128.710 / 131.175 / 132.204 Hz, loss 0.418. Raising body 1's tuning 1 % moves mode 1 by 1.205 Hz and the others by less than 0.1; raising body 0's tuning 1 % moves modes 2 and 3 by 0.37 and 0.95 Hz; doubling the 0–2 coupling moves mode 3 alone by 1.09 Hz; doubling the 0–1 coupling moves all three by 0.13–0.18 Hz; raising one mass 10 % moves the frequencies by at most 0.055 Hz and raising all masses leaves them unchanged (scale invariance); doubling the loss changes only the damping rate. So: the three frequencies and the decay are set by the three tunings, the couplings and the loss, with the masses nearly invisible to the spectrum; the map from the 6 to 7 shape parameters to the 3 frequencies has rank at most 3, which means **every reachable spectrum is reached by a family of anatomies, not one**. That is not a defect of the plan; it is the plan's content. The anatomies in that family differ in what the spectrum does not fix: the mode vectors, hence where the energy goes under a given strike (the envelope shares measured for the family study ranged from 0.47/0.09/0.44 to 0.13/0.73/0.14 for nearly equal spectra).

## Recommended scope: the network, not the beam, and a visible failed fit
1. **Target**: a declared synthetic specification `{ normalFrequenciesHz: [f1, f2, f3], decaySeconds, attack: 'the page strike on body 01' }` authored by hand and stated as synthetic; a second target with ratios the family cannot reach (for example 1 : 2 : 3) for the failed-fit demonstration.
2. **Objective**: least squares in cents between the target's and the candidate's damped normal frequencies plus the relative decay error, over tunings (within the cluster bound), couplings (within bounds) and loss, with masses fixed equal (unidentifiable from the spectrum and said so) and the topology fixed per candidate (chain for one, triangle for the other). Bounded coordinate descent or Nelder–Mead with deterministic seeded restarts in dependency-free JS, every iterate validated through `validateGrove` so passivity, stability and the model's bounds hold at every step; the residual, the iterations and the restarts in the report.
3. **Two candidates**: the best chain and the best triangle for the same target; if both reach the target within a stated tolerance (say 2 cents and 5 % decay), the workshop has produced two bodies with one declared sound.
4. **Falsifying test, the same unfitted gestures for both**: the page strike on each body and the firm hand, as in the body study; compare the envelope shares and the per-body peaks; the claim "two bodies, one sound" is falsified if their point responses differ by less than the fit tolerance's effect on them (then the inverse problem was not underdetermined in any way that matters), and "the same sound" is falsified if their spectra differ by more than the tolerance. The report states both numbers; no listening verdict.
5. **The failed fit**: the 1 : 2 : 3 target is refused with the best residual shown (a uniform beam cannot either, analytically: its ratios are fixed), which is the honest half of inverse design: most sounds have no body in this family.
6. **Does it add beyond an analytical solve?** For the beam, nothing: the solution is one combination and the twins are identical, so the beam appears only as the ambiguity example with no fit. For the network, yes: three coupled nonlinear equations under bounds with a solution family, which is exactly where "possible bodies" becomes true and inspectable.

## Not claimed
That the fitted bodies sound different (a listening pair can be rendered from kept records through the existing path, with levels reported, and the question left open), that any real instrument is reconstructed, or that the target is a measurement. Existing engine files stay unchanged; the fit, the targets and the comparison would live in new score-side files under Fable's ownership once Astra constrains the experiment.

## Addendum: Astra's constructive ambiguity witness, checked independently (2026-11-02)
Astra's probe (equal masses 0.02 kg, uncoupled 220 / 222 / 225 Hz, bridges 1–2 500, 1–3 300, 2–3 400 N/m, γ 0.7) rotates coordinates 2 and 3 by an angle θ, K' = Q K Qᵀ, and reads the result back as ground springs and bridges. I rebuilt it from the stiffness matrix with my own code against the project's `coupledNetwork` and `networkStrikeResponse`: poles 221.3166 / 225.0533 / 227.4171 Hz for the base and for every admitted rotation; θ = −20° gives bridges 572.5 / 110.9 / 30.4 (admitted), θ = +30° gives 283.0 / 509.8 / 571.9 (admitted), θ = −30° gives a negative 2–3 bridge (−171.9) and is refused, exactly as Astra found; the collocated response (strike 1, pickup 1) agrees to relative RMS 2.9e-13 and 4.1e-13 over 3 s at 8 kHz, and the held-out responses (strike 2 → pickup 1 and, by reciprocity, 1 → 2) differ by 8.9 % and 33.9 %. Why, in one line: the first row of Q is e₁ᵀ, so every mode vector keeps its first component and the collocated sum Σ v_r[1]² g_r(t) is invariant, while the cross sum Σ v_r[1] v_r[2] g_r(t) mixes components 2 and 3. The witness is constructed from the known model, not inferred from audio or found by a search, and it is the right foundation: the same poles and the same whole one-gesture waveform, a different response to an unfitted gesture, in an actual passive lumped body. Read back into the Grove's own parameterisation (coupling = k / (m ω²), cluster within 40 cents): the +30° twin is a Grove within `GROVE_BOUNDS` as it stands; the −20° twin's 2–3 bridge (0.0008) falls under the 0.001 floor, so a witness pair inside the current bounds should use angles like +30°, or the floor is the first thing the experiment's constraints must decide. Scope consequence: the workshop's two bodies can be built by this transform rather than by an optimiser, with the fit reserved for the failed-fit demonstration against an unreachable target.
