# Assessment: from a local sound file to a declared target (Fable, 2026-11-02; constraints and a minimal honest study scope; no implementation in shared modules until ownership is agreed)

Astra's direction: a bounded local path, sound file → inspect spectral evidence → the visitor confirms a declared target → the existing inverse search. Not the microphone or the Voice Observatory (planning only), not automatic unique-body recovery, not whole-sound matching. The founding note's first experiment (initial-idea.md §8) starts exactly here: "a metallic, decaying note with a handful of clear partials" as the target, a family of shapes, a search for a close response, the performance animated from simulated state, then exploration.

## Constraints already in force, which the path must keep
1. **Local only.** Runtime stays on 127.0.0.1; nothing is uploaded; a chosen file is read as selected bytes with a stated bound (the kept-file reader's 16 KiB is for JSON; a sound file needs its own bound, for example 10 s and 4 MiB, stated on the page).
2. **What the search fits.** Three damped normal frequencies and one common decay (INVERSE_FITTED); excluded by declaration are mode amplitudes, pickup positions, attack and strike position, masses, beam partials and "anything heard" (INVERSE_EXCLUDED). A sound file can therefore only ever yield a *declared target*: three frequencies and a decay that the visitor confirms; the file's loudness, attack, timbre and any partial beyond the three are not fitted, and the page must say so where it says "no audio upload" today (the catalogue line changes from "no audio upload" to "a local sound file gives only a declared three-resonance target").
3. **Family bounds.** Bodies tune within 80 to 600 Hz; the three resonances of a candidate are bounded by the family ratio (1.128 for equal masses, 1.342 unequal). A file whose clear partials lie above 600 Hz, or whose ratio exceeds the bound, is reported as outside the family, never forced in.
4. **Provenance.** `validateInverseTarget` requires a provenance sentence; an estimated target's provenance names the file (name, duration, byte length, SHA-256 of the bytes), the estimator and its resolution, and the visitor's edits.
5. **Honest claims.** No "match" claim and no "recovered original": a search result is an anatomy whose three resonances approximate the declared three within tolerance; the inverse workshop's existing cards and limits carry that.
6. **Browser evidence.** Any spectral analysis run in the browser reads samples through Web Audio decoding; Brave returns such data uniformly scaled (measured 0.9997) under its fingerprint defences, which leaves frequencies untouched but biases levels; exact evidence runs in Safari, and the page should not report levels as facts about the file.

## Minimal scientifically honest offline study (Node, no browser, no shared-module edits)
Purpose: measure what a simple estimator can and cannot recover before any page exists, on sounds whose true resonances are known.
- **Known-answer inputs, generated by the project itself**: (a) a single strike of the kept chain rendered by `createInverseInstrument` (its three normal frequencies and decay are known exactly); (b) the Grove voice's rendered strike for seed 3 (three low modes plus six beam partials at 800 Hz and above: a "handful of clear partials" case where the low three must be told from the partials); (c) a synthetic sum of three decaying sines at chosen frequencies and decays (resolution and decay-fit accuracy under control); (d) negative controls: a four-resonance synthetic, a note whose peaks exceed 600 Hz, and white noise.
- **Estimator (study only)**: windowed spectrum over the first 0.5 to 1 s with quadratic peak interpolation for frequency; decay from a linear fit of log band-amplitude over successive frames per peak; a common decay as the amplitude-weighted mean with its spread reported; a family check (count of clear peaks at or below 600 Hz, ratio bound).
- **Measurements to report**: for (a) and (b), the estimated three frequencies against the known ones in cents and the estimated decay against the known one in percent, as a function of window length (0.25, 0.5, 1 s) and of added noise (−40, −30 dB); for (c), the smallest frequency spacing the estimator separates at each window length (the three default resonances lie 2.3 and 1.0 Hz apart, which a 0.5 s window cannot resolve: the study must show at what window length they separate, since the default target is exactly such a cluster); for (d), that the family check refuses or flags rather than fitting.
- **Deliverable**: a tool under my ownership (`tools/study-sound-file-target.mjs`) printing its definitions with its tables, and a note of results; no estimator in `src/` until the study says what it can honestly do. The decisive number is the resolution: if the estimator cannot separate resonances closer than a few hertz without a window longer than the note, the page must let the visitor declare a single resonance and a decay and leave the other two to the search's family rather than inventing them.

## What the page would then be (for agreement, Astra's ownership)
Open a local sound file (bounded); show the spectrum of its first second with the estimated peaks and decay as numbers with their resolution; the visitor confirms or edits three frequencies and a decay into the existing target fields (provenance filled from the file); the existing search runs unchanged. No playback of the file is needed for the path; if offered, it is the visitor's explicit choice. The founding experiment's last step, exploring the invented body with a phrase, already exists.
