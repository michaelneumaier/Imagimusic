THE SHAPE OF A RELATIONSHIP

Nothing here
rings entirely alone.

The visible tendrils belong to the sound model. A different grove brings different masses, tunings and connections. Try a single touch, a shared gesture, or a little silence. There is no particular feeling to reach.

What is physical, and what is imagined?

Three lumped masses and springs generate the sound. All gestures join that same linear system. It has three normal modes, one shared damping rate, and no acoustic radiation or room simulation. The sound is a stereo mixture of displacement pickups, placed across the stereo field by the bodies’ horizontal layout. Camera movement changes only the view.

Shell volume follows mass; tuning changes shell proportions without changing that volume. A tendril exists only for a modeled connection, and its thickness maps coupling on an artistic scale. The support, suspension, fluted shells, materials and layout are design. They do not claim hollow-shell acoustics or a simulated support beam. Shell width and glow amplify each body’s response envelope, not audio-rate movement or energy flow. A pale ring around a held body shows your pending gesture; it is an input cue. A brief warm ring marks the location of a recorded strike, including during the greeting and replay; it does not depict vibration or energy transfer. Drag empty space to turn the view.

The optional voice gives each body three beam-family partials coupled at their attachment points. Each normal mode has an assigned decay rate using a classical-damping approximation. Width and glow show only the coherent low-frequency band below 1.5 times the lowest body tuning. Strike positions are three discrete controls, mapped artistically from the upper shell toward the lower bridge attachment; they do not describe this shell’s acoustic geometry. One normalization is shared across every body and strike position. Saved gestures include the complete voice configuration and the selected position in a distinct voice-capture format.

The sound level is fixed per grove. Very dense input may need a moment of space to preserve headroom; captures hold up to 128 gestures over two minutes. Quiet records a short ending fade.
