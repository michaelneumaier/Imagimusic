THE NORTH STAR

Watching should help
explain hearing.

Michael’s original idea begins with the wonder of Animusic: music embodied by strange machines. Imagimusic explores a further possibility—an invented body whose simulated behavior actually produces the sound.

A place for attention

The spiritual and psychedelic direction lives in the encounter: recurrence that changes how you listen, a touch that changes what remains, an unexpected answer across a connection. There is no prescribed feeling to achieve.

Two kinds of truth

Some instruments use physical models of vibrating bodies. Others interpret synthesizer controls as visible mechanisms. Artistic geometry and amplified motion are identified as such. These are bounded models, not full acoustic objects or claims about consciousness.

  1. OCT 31, 2026

    The seed

    An imagined physical body for electronic sound. The first resonator garden and a shared collaboration protocol.

  2. NOV 1 · FOUNDATIONS

    From models to a stage

    Physical tine, interpreted choir, shared state and score, mechanical performers, MIDI mapping, and local audio/video rendering.

  3. NOV 1 · ENCOUNTERS

    From watching to touching

    Coupled bodies become playable Groves. Rest, powered gestures, keeping and reopening passages, and structural adaptation make the response something to explore.

  4. NOV 1 · RELATIONSHIPS

    From response to relationship

    An unfinished phrase invites an answer. Listening studies compare space, recurrence, and three harmonic centers. The first ensemble prototype brings three intact Groves together.

  5. NOV 2 · PLAYING TOGETHER

    A way into three voices

    Adjustable listening levels, a saved-ensemble reader and an optional demonstration make the prototype easier to enter and return to. Direct replay and a reader for the full two-minute passage complete the return path within its stated file limits. Your turn starts a fresh passage; authored examples remain separate.

  6. NOV 2 · RETURNING

    The same touches, other voices

    A finished passage can become a separate variation: C’s gestures move to E♭, E♭’s to G, and G’s to C. The original stays intact. Prepare, listen and keep are distinct choices; saved returns carry their declared source identity.

  7. NOV 2 · CONTINUING

    A memory becomes a beginning

    A short finished passage can now open a new performance on the same instruments. The audio engine confirms the handoff before live controls become available. The original stays separate; continued gestures carry their remembered history, and the reader can reopen the result. Native desktop-browser checks cover the playing and keeping path; device performance and the experience of listening remain open questions.

  8. NOV 2 · LOOKING CLOSER

    An instrument, up close

    An optional close view brings one intact Grove into closer view while all three remain audible and playable. Together restores the full picture. This makes the forms and hand marks easier to inspect; whether individual response pulses are clear enough remains an open visual question.

  9. NOV 2 · FOLLOWING A VOICE

    A changing chord, seen closely

    The expressive study now offers a fixed close view of a chosen voice. The picture, inspector, and waiting or finished state follow the same identity while the whole passage remains audible. A bounded local MIDI 2.0 Clip importer brings supported notes and expression into this synthesizer interpretation.

  10. NOV 2 · POSSIBLE ANATOMIES

    From resonances to playable candidates

    A bounded inverse search now proposes chain and triangle layouts for a small declared target. Each candidate carries its residuals and can be played through the same three-mass model used in fitting. Matching resonances leaves other aspects of the response open to discovery; it does not recover a unique acoustic body.
